Snake Pit

Every snake here is playing snake against all the others, in the same box. None of them were trained. Each one has a few numbers I call genes that decide how it plays, and the ones that play well enough to split pass slightly changed copies on to their kids.

the rules
  1. Every step, every snake moves one square. Eating an egg makes it one square longer.
  2. Moving costs energy. Each time a snake has spent a full unit, its tail tip dies. That's starving: no food means it shrinks to nothing. A dead tail tip leaves a faint shed skin and no food.
  3. Running into a wall or any body, its own or someone else's, kills it. A snake that dies leaves one egg behind.
  4. The longer a snake gets, the more likely it splits in half. The back half turns around, so its old tail tip becomes its new head.
how a snake decides

Each step a snake has three choices: turn left, go straight, or turn right. It scores each one and takes the best. The score adds a pull toward the nearest egg it can see, a push away from any wall or body it can see straight ahead, and a bit of randomness.

Six genes set those weights: appetite is how hard it pulls toward food, sight is how far away it can see an egg, caution is how hard it avoids danger, wariness is how far ahead it sees danger, maturity is the length where splitting gets likely, and jitter is the randomness.

brains cost food

The first four genes are the snake's brain, and running a brain costs energy. A snake with sharp senses and strong drives starves faster than a dull one, so evolution has to trade better play against needing more food. A snake's color comes from its genes, so a family stays roughly one color until it drifts.
