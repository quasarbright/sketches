Hilbert Portrait

The whole picture is one line. It never lifts, never crosses itself, and visits every part of the sheet. Up close it's a maze of right-angle turns; from across the room it's the photo.

the curve

A Hilbert curve is built by recursion. Start with a square and a path that walks its four quarters in a U: in at the bottom-left, out at the bottom-right. Now replace each quarter with a smaller copy of the whole U, rotated or flipped so that each copy's exit lands right next to the following copy's entrance. Repeat. After $n$ steps the path visits a $2^n \times 2^n$ grid of cells, every one exactly once, as a single unbroken line.

tone from depth

Because every copy keeps the same entrance and exit, nothing forces all the quarters to recurse equally far. A light patch can stop early and be crossed by a few long strokes; a dark patch can keep subdividing until it's packed with tight wiggles. The pieces still join end to end, so it's still one line.

Where a big cell meets a small one their centres don't line up, so the line doesn't go through centres. Instead it picks where to cross each shared edge, so every cell holds either a straight run or a single right-angle turn, just like a cell of an ordinary Hilbert curve. A cell the line runs straight through needs both crossings at the same height, and it can always get them: both neighbours sit against the same side of it, so the smaller one's edge fits inside the larger one's. Where every cell is the same size, this lands every turn at a cell centre: the classic curve exactly.

A cell of side $s$ gets roughly one line per $s$ of width, so with line width $w$ it is inked to about $w/s$. To make a patch with darkness $D$ (0 = white, 1 = black), the cell size should be $s = s_{\min}/D$, where $s_{\min}$ is the finest cell. That's a recursion depth of

$$d = d_{\max} + \log_2 D.$$

So every halving of darkness costs one level of recursion. The sketch works out that target depth for every pixel, then splits a cell whenever the average target under it is deeper than the cell is. Contrast bends the tone curve before that ($D \leftarrow D^{\gamma}$), and floor stops white areas from going completely empty.

wide and tall photos

A Hilbert curve fills a square, but its entrance and exit sit on the same side. Several squares side by side can therefore be chained exit to entrance, so a landscape photo becomes a row of squares and a portrait one becomes a column. It's still one line.
