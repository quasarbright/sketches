Wormhole

Two universes joined by a tunnel. Each one here is just a sky: a pattern painted on a sphere infinitely far away. Universe A is where you start; universe B is through the hole.

the shape of the space

Picture a funnel whose narrow end is glued to the narrow end of a second funnel. A two-dimensional creature living on that surface could walk down one funnel, through the ring where they meet, and up into the other, never leaving the surface. Far from the ring each funnel flattens out into an ordinary plane.

The wormhole is the same thing one dimension up. Every sphere around the hole has some area, and the closer you get the smaller those spheres are, down to a smallest one, the throat. Past it, the spheres grow again, but now you're in the other universe.

The picture in the top-left corner is that funnel seen edge-on, with a dot for where you are. Its horizontal width is the radius r of the sphere you're on; going down the picture is going through.

To describe where you are you need one number for the distance ℓ along the tunnel (positive in A, negative in B, zero at the throat) and a direction on the sphere. The whole shape is set by how the sphere's radius depends on ℓ:

$$ ds^2 = d\ell^2 + r(\ell)^2\,(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\, d\varphi^2) $$

This uses the r(ℓ) from the paper the Interstellar team published about rendering their wormhole: a tube of radius throat radius and length throat length, flaring out into flat space over a distance set by flare.

how light moves

Light goes as straight as it can, which on a curved surface means the shortest path. A ray aimed straight at the hole goes through. A ray aimed off to the side bends around the throat and comes back out into the universe it started in, in a different direction. A ray aimed just past the edge of the throat circles it before escaping, and every other ray bends somewhere in between.

So looking at the hole you see all of universe B squeezed into a disk, and around the disk you see your own sky smeared into rings, including the part of it that's behind you.

how it runs in real time

The space is perfectly round, so any ray stays in the flat slice that contains it and the center of the hole. Inside that slice, where the ray ends up depends on only two numbers: where you are (ℓ) and the angle between the ray and the direction straight away from the hole.

So once a frame, the GPU traces 4096 rays in a single slice at your ℓ, at angles from straight out to straight in, each stepped through the curved space until it's far enough out that space is flat again. It stores which universe each ray ended up in and which direction it was heading. Then for each pixel it measures that one angle, looks up the answer, and turns it back to the pixel's own slice. The skies are drawn from formulas rather than images, so they stay sharp however much the throat magnifies them.

flying

You fly with the same rule light uses. When you coast, you move in the straightest possible line through the curved space, and your view turns with the space so that flying through the throat carries you into B the right way up.
