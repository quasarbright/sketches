Jumping Pegs

A puzzle from 3Blue1Brown's monthly series with MoMath. Three pegs sit at three corners of a square, on an ordinary continuous plane — no grid.

A move takes one peg and jumps it over another: it lands the same distance past the peg it jumped, along whatever line the two pegs happen to define. The peg jumped over doesn't move. You can jump along any of the three pairs, in either direction, as many times as you like.

The question: can you ever get a peg onto the fourth corner of the square? If so, what's the fewest moves it takes. If not, why not.

The faint dashed lines are sightlines — a record of every jump, start through pivot to landing point — left on the board as you play.
