mini ksp

An orbital mechanics sandbox with no rocket — just a ship, a planet (Vesper), a moon (Lume), and Kepler. Everything counterintuitive about orbits lives here: to catch up to something, slow down; to go faster at the far side, burn at the near side.

the model

Motion is one two-body problem at a time. Near Vesper you're on a conic (ellipse or hyperbola) around Vesper; inside Lume's sphere of influence — the region where the moon's gravity dominates, drawn as the dashed circle — you're on a conic around Lume. The path is these conics patched together at the boundary. Each conic obeys vis-viva, $v^2 = \mu\left(\frac{2}{r} - \frac{1}{a}\right)$, and is solved in closed form: no simulation steps, just Kepler's equation. Burns are instant velocity changes that fire automatically at their planned time.

why time travel is free

Because everything is closed-form, your position is a pure function of the clock and the flight plan. Warp, rewind, and jump don't fast-forward a simulation — they just move the clock. Rewinding to before a burn un-executes it, and the future re-forms the moment you edit the plan.

flying
  • Click your trajectory to place a burn; it snaps to Ap/Pe when close.
  • Drag the yellow handles for prograde/retrograde, cyan for radial. Hold shift while dragging for fine control.
  • Select Lume as target to see your closest approach; raise your orbit until the paths kiss.
  • Scroll to zoom, drag to pan; the camera stays on the ship.
